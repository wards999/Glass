# 0021 — NACK: ask again for what FEC can't rebuild

**Status:** accepted · **Date:** 2026-11-23

## Context
FEC (ADR 0020 step 1) rebuilds scattered losses but not Wi-Fi's bursts.
With FEC alone, losssim lost 0.38 % of frames in mild bursts and 2 % in
heavy ones. Each lost frame costs a keyframe request: a visible hitch of a
round trip, a large IDR and its transfer. The round trip on a home network
is 3–4 ms, so asking for the missing packets again is far cheaper.

## Decision
- **Viewer** (`packet::Reassembler`):
  - A frame that FEC can't complete is *stalled* once a newer frame's
    packets arrive, or none of its own came for 3 ms (the last frame
    before a still screen).
  - It sends a **NACK** datagram (`"GLNK"`, protocol v11) at once,
    sealed with its key like input, over UDP. File copies share the TCP
    connection, so NACKs don't go there. A NACK asks for only as many
    packets per FEC group as the group still lacks. A frame that never
    showed at all is asked for whole.
  - It asks again every 1.5 round trips (at least 4 ms) and gives up
    4 round trips + 10 ms after the first ask (20–60 ms). Giving up means
    the old path: wait for a keyframe.
  - Newer frames wait meanwhile (a reorder window of up to 16 frames), so
    frames still go to the decoder in order. A newer IDR ends the wait at
    once.
  - The receive loop wakes every 2 ms while a frame is incomplete.
- **Host** (`packet::Packetiser`):
  - Keeps the last 32 frames' payloads (buffers swapped, never copied) and
    resends from them for up to 150 ms. Resends are flagged `RESENT` (bit 2
    of the header flags).
  - It answers on the UDP thread, so a still screen (no new frames) is no
    reason to wait.
  - One sealer serves both frames and resends: its counter is the nonce
    and must never repeat. It sits behind a mutex that is contended only
    while a NACK is being answered.
- Resent packets don't count as having arrived in the loss report, so FEC
  still follows the real loss.

## Consequences
- In losssim (4 ms round trip), frames lost in mild bursts went from 23 to
  0, and in heavy bursts from 124 to 11. The cost is a few ms on the frames
  that needed it: p99 0–8 ms, worst 25 ms. A keyframe hitch costs more.
- Up to 16 frames' buffers on the viewer (256 KB each to start), and 32
  frames' payloads on the host.
- LATER(M4): pacing packets on their own thread (the rest of step 2). M5's
  rate control will count resends in its budget.
