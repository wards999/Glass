# 0032 — The Windows host talks to NVENC directly

**Status:** accepted · **Date:** 2026-11-24 · M8's direct NVENC path, brought forward

## Context
Glass encoded through FFmpeg's `hevc_nvenc`. FFmpeg restarts the encoder
with a keyframe at every rate-control change (`nvenc.c` sets `resetEncoder`
and `forceIDR` for any bitrate or buffer change) and has no per-frame
quality. So:
- each bitrate step cost a keyframe (m5-test10: 68 IDRs for 63 changes);
- a still photo couldn't be sent sharp: the encoder's quality follows its
  per-frame share of the bitrate (80 Mbps / 165 fps), re-sending the same
  picture adds ~0.1 KB, and a temporary budget change forced a coarse
  keyframe after it (m5-test22..24: blurry photos, then a blur cycle).

## Decision
- `app-win/src/win/nvenc.rs` drives NVENC through NVIDIA's API
  (`nvEncodeAPI64.dll`, API 12.1: drivers 531+), same input (BGRA texture,
  GPU copy) and output (Annex B, BT.601 limited range in the VUI, SPS/PPS
  on keyframes) as before: P1 preset, ultra-low-latency tuning, one pass,
  CBR with a tenth of a second of buffer, no B-frames, no periodic
  keyframes.
- Rate control changes use `nvEncReconfigureEncoder` without a reset or a
  keyframe.
- A still picture (200 ms after a change worth it) is sent once as a
  keyframe at a fixed QP: 20 with room (no limit, or ≥ 60 Mbps), 24 at
  20–60 Mbps, 28 below; then rate control carries on, no keyframe after.
- Bindings: `nvenc_sys.rs`, generated by `scripts/nvenc-bindings.ps1` from
  `nvEncodeAPI.h` (nv-codec-headers n12.1.14.1, MIT, pinned by SHA-256),
  committed so builds need neither the header nor bindgen.
- FFmpeg's path stays as the fallback (and `GLASS_ENCODER=ffmpeg`), and for
  recording.

## Measured (QUIETPC22, `glass-cli nvenc-test`, a grainy 2560x1440 picture)
- Bitrate change: 0.1 KB delta frame, no keyframe. After the quality
  keyframe: 0.1 KB, no keyframe. All frames decode (FFmpeg).
- 4:2:0: keyframe 534 KB → quality keyframe 1450 KB (green 27.1 → 29.6 dB).
- 4:4:4: 563 KB, 26.2 dB → 2241 KB, 31.4 dB.
- Live (local host + probe): 383 frames, encode p50 6.5 ms p99 8.2 ms,
  capture → received p50 10.5 ms.

## Later
Reference-frame invalidation after a loss instead of a keyframe (ADR 0022)
now fits here too.

## Addendum (m5-test27): a quality floor while the link has room
m5-test26: Fast stills went sharp after ~1 s; "4:4:4 never goes sharp";
Windows Remote Desktop is sharp at once (it sends still areas with lossless
or near-lossless codecs, video only where things move). Glass now puts a
floor under every frame's quality (max QP 24) while rate control sees no
limit, and lifts it as soon as the viewer limits the bitrate (no reset, no
keyframe). Test picture (grainy 2560x1440, 4:4:4): first frame 563 KB /
26.2 dB → 1309 KB / 28.2 dB, and the next frames keep adding detail
(125 KB, 50 KB) before the still keyframe (31.4 dB). Ordinary desktop use
unchanged (frames p50 36 KB). The results now list every encoder a session
started, its colour, and why the direct path didn't start if it didn't
(the 4:4:4 encoder is a new one: a fallback to FFmpeg's path there would
explain m5-test26).

## Addendum (m5-test28): floor removed, sharpening sooner
m5-test27 (QUIETPC, RTX 2060, 4:4:4 over the eero mesh hop): stills went
sharp, but scrolling through photos stalled and then jumped. The floor
applied to every frame, scrolling ones too: frames up to 850 KB, encode p99
48 ms, max 186 ms. It's gone; frames in motion are small again and the
still keyframe does the sharpening. That keyframe now comes 300 ms after the
picture stops (was 400 ms) and may follow the last one after 0.5 s (was
2 s, which made the next photo wait the rest of the 2 s).

## Addendum (m5-test29): the 4:4:4 viewer showed every picture one late
m5-test28: 57 still keyframes sent and received, yet 4:4:4 "never cleared".
`glass-cli decode444-test` (the encoder's test stream, one access unit at a
time): `hevc_cuvid` gave 5 pictures for 6 frames. It leaves the end of a
picture to NVIDIA's parser, which only knows when the next one starts, so
each picture came out a frame late and a still screen's last one, the sharp
keyframe, never did (m5-test27's floor hid it: the blurry frames before were
sharp enough). The viewer now uses FFmpeg's own HEVC decoder on NVDEC (same
GPU, CUDA frames, no frame threads): 6 of 6, each at once. This also takes a
frame of delay off every 4:4:4 picture, and the jump at the end of a scroll.

## Addendum (m5-test32): the quality frame is a correction, not a keyframe
m5-test29/30: stills sharp after ~0.5 s (300 ms wait, then a full keyframe
at QP 20 to encode on QUIETPC's RTX 2060 and send over the eero mesh hop).
The quality frame is now an ordinary frame at a fixed QP: it corrects the
picture the viewer has. `nvenc-test --image` (grainy 2560x1440, PSNR against
the original): 4:4:4 keyframe QP 20 2241 KB / 36.9 dB → correction QP 18
1920 KB / 37.2 dB; 4:2:0 1450 KB / 40.2 dB → 1098 KB / 40.4 dB. As sharp,
15-25 % smaller (less than hoped: the blurry picture before it is a poor
reference); encode time unchanged (11 ms / 7 ms on the RTX 4070). The wait
is now 200 ms. Losing it costs nothing extra: the viewer asks for a
keyframe after any loss, as before.

Also: the host's results split encode times into 4:4:4, 4:2:0 and quality
frames, and the viewer's say, per colour, how many pictures were decoded and
shown and how often two or more were dropped at once: m5-test30's card
animations went missing in 4:4:4 only, with the network and queue clean.
