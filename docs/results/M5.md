# M5 — Smoothness: results

Status: **in progress.** Step 1 (the frame-rate cap) is built (m5-test1).

## Why it started early (m4b-test2)
The Mac (60 Hz) viewing QUIETPC22 (165 Hz) in a Steam game, 299 s:
- 75 fps arrived and 37.5 fps were shown: each frame was held for two
  refreshes, and `present` took 32 ms.
- capture → screen was 53 ms p50, so the sound (30 ms) came early.
- The decode queue was full 25 times: 39 keyframe requests, and 5 hitches
  averaging 138 ms.

## Step 1: at most one frame per refresh of the viewer's screen
- The host sends at the refresh the viewer reports in `Hello`, never more
  than its own screen's:
  - **Windows** (in-app and through the service): a frame that comes too
    soon is kept (a GPU copy) and sent when its turn comes; a newer one
    replaces it.
  - **Mac:** the newest frame waits in the encode queue the same way.
- The interval is 95 % of the viewer's refresh, so a new frame arrives for
  every refresh.
- The host's results say "frame rate (M5): at most N fps".
- Expected: the Mac shows close to 60 fps, the picture's delay falls, and
  the sound follows it down (its A/V hold, m4b-test3).

### m5-test1 (the Mac viewing QUIETPC22, 3D pool, 195 s)
- Better:
  - present interval p50 16.67 ms (was 33.3): a new frame every refresh
    when frames come;
  - queue full 0 (was 25), keyframe requests 7 (was 39), hitches 3 at
    30 ms (were 5 at 138 ms);
  - sound concealed 2 with 0 gaps (was 45 and 11).
- Worse: the user said "the pool balls felt less smooth this time". The PC
  sent only 29 fps (1,780 of 3,528 frames held back). Windows' default
  timer is 15.6 ms, so the cap's "send the held frame in 1 ms" took 16 ms.
  The Mac received 45 fps.
- Unchanged: capture → screen 54 ms. 32 ms of it is `present` on the Mac:
  a frame presented at the view's display link reaches the screen two
  refreshes later (the window manager composites it at the next refresh,
  then it's shown).
- The sound followed the picture, 24 ms behind (78 against 54). That's
  slow to settle while the backlog drains.

Fixed in m5-test2:
- **Windows:** 1 ms timer resolution for the session (`timeBeginPeriod`,
  per process on Windows 11). The cap now sends at 60. The sound capture,
  which also woke only every 16 ms, wakes every 2 ms.
- **Mac:**
  - frames are presented through `CAMetalDisplayLink` with
    `preferredFrameLatency = 1` (macOS 14), one refresh instead of two;
  - the layer is opaque, so full screen can skip compositing;
  - the results say "presented by: …".
- **Sound:** catching up a backlog is twice as quick (up to 2 %).

### m5-test2 (the Mac viewing QUIETPC22, 3D pool, ~230 s)
- The PC sent 54 fps (was 29); 53 arrived and 50 were shown (were 45 and
  43).
- Sound: 0 concealed and 0 gaps in 200 s.
- `present` was still 32 ms with `CAMetalDisplayLink` at one frame of
  latency: no gain. capture → screen stayed at 54 ms.
- The sound was 34 ms behind (88 against 54). Its target came down only
  5 ms every 10 s after a rise.
- "A few stutters as the first break occurred": frames up to 143 KB (p50
  33), encode up to 47 ms, and 57 Wi-Fi spikes over 8 ms (worst 54 ms
  over). Pacing and rate control (M5) are for that.

Changed in m5-test3:
- **Mac:** each frame is presented as soon as it's decoded, not at the
  display link's tick just after a refresh, with up to two on their way
  and the newest waiting if more come. The window manager picks it up at
  the next refresh. Expected: `present` and "wait for refresh" together
  down from ~41 ms to ~25 ms. The Metal display link is gone; "presented
  by: as decoded".
- **Both hosts:** the cap's interval is 90 % of the viewer's refresh (was
  95 %), so a frame is always ready for each refresh. An extra one is
  superseded on the viewer.
- **Sound:** the loss margin and the picture-sync hold are kept apart, so
  a change in the hold takes effect at once. A test raising the hold to
  70 ms then dropping it to 20 ms: back under 35 ms within 4 s, with no
  gap.

### The user: "it actually seemed smoother before we started trying to match the 60 fps"
Why:
- **Uncapped (m4b-test2):** 165 fps came in, so at each refresh the Mac
  had a fresh frame. Every picture was sampled at almost the same point,
  and motion moved in even steps.
- **Capped at the viewer's refresh (m5-test1 to 3):** the PC chose frames
  on its own clock, which drifts against the Mac's screen. Some refreshes
  got a fresh frame and some an old one, now and then two in one refresh
  and none in the next. That's uneven steps: judder, even at 60 fps.

Changed in m5-test4:
- **Hosts send at twice the viewer's refresh** (120 for a 60 Hz Mac).
  There's always a fresh frame, without 165 fps's load.
- **The Mac presents the newest frame 5 ms before each refresh.** At
  every refresh it schedules the presentation for 5 ms before the next
  one, then shows whatever is newest. That's even sampling, and it shows
  sooner than presenting at the start of a refresh. This replaces m5-test3's
  "as decoded".
- LATER(M5): lock the host's sending to the viewer's refresh (the viewer
  tells the host when its screen refreshes, through clock sync), so twice
  isn't needed.

### m5-test4 (the Mac viewing QUIETPC22, 3D pool, 220 s)

| | m5-test2 | m5-test4 |
|---|---|---|
| present p50 | 32.4 ms | **20.4 ms** |
| capture → screen p50 | 53.9 ms | **39.5 ms** |
| input → screen p50 | 65.6 ms | **49.2 ms** |
| encode on the host p99 / max | 17.5 / 46.9 ms | **4.1 / 28.8 ms** |
| sent / shown | 54 / 50 fps | 94.7 / 51.5 fps |
| sound vs picture | 34 ms behind | 18 ms behind |

- **51.5 shown of 60:** when a late present ran after the next refresh's
  tick, that tick saw it pending and skipped.
- **The sound was 18 ms behind** because of its loss margin, not the
  picture: 5 concealed and 1 gap had added about 35 ms, coming down 5 ms
  per 10 s.
- The bitrate rose to 36–43 Mbps with twice the frames (rate control's
  job on weaker Wi-Fi).

Changed in m5-test5:
- Every refresh schedules its own present.
- The sound's loss margin grows by 2 ms per concealed frame (was 5) and
  5 ms per gap (was 10), and comes down every 5 s (was 10).
- losssim: still no gaps. Concealment in bursts is up (1.1 %: 18 frames,
  was 9) for half the buffer (27 ms, was 45).

### Worst moments (m5-test6)
The user, on 3D pool: "the first change still felt off", from the static
view to the live table. Totals over minutes can't show one moment, so the
results now list the worst ones, with their time into the session:
- **Mac viewer:** the 6 longest gaps on screen after the first 2 s. Each
  gives the frame that ended it: its size, whether it was a keyframe, its
  network time and its capture → screen.
- **PC host:**
  - the 5 longest pauses in capture while the picture was moving (frames
    had been coming within 30 ms, then nothing for over 40 ms: the game,
    or capture);
  - the 5 biggest frames, with their encode time.

Matching the times on the two sides (a second or so apart: each counts
from its own session start) says whether a moment came from the game or
capture, the encoder, the network, or the Mac.

### m5-test6: the "first change" was the game loading
- The PC's capture paused for 0.2–0.4 s five times between 12 and 21 s,
  around the switch from the table list to the game. The Mac's gaps lined
  up (283–633 ms), with normal network times (4–13 ms) and normal capture →
  screen (35–52 ms) for the frames that ended them.
- The user saw a pause on the PC itself there: the game loading. Steam's
  FPS counter can confirm it.
- Otherwise: capture → screen 36.5 ms, present p99 32.7 ms, input → screen
  49 ms, no frames lost.

## Pacing (m5-test7, ADR 0026)
- Each frame is spread over at most half the viewer's refresh (8.3 ms at
  60 Hz), never slower than 4× the recent average: about 2 ms for a typical
  47 KB frame, at most 8 ms for a 335 KB scene change.
- The first packets go at once, and a thread sends the rest on time.
- Newest first: the rest of a frame goes at once when a newer one is
  queued.
- Audio, input, resends and keep-alives bypass it.
- Tests: the queue spreads, hurries and fills as it should; 138 core tests
  pass, including the real host → viewer loopback.

### m5-test7 (pacing, 3D pool, 240–270 s)
- **Pacing did what it was set to:** frames spread over 2.4 ms on average
  (longest 10.3 ms), 19 hurried for a newer frame.
- **The Wi-Fi spikes were smaller:** 30 over 8 ms, worst 17 ms over (were
  15, worst 49).
- capture → screen 38.9 ms, input → screen 49.3 ms, no frames lost.
- **But 1,142 false NACKs:** 0 frames saved, 36,964 duplicate packets,
  32,587 resent, about 1.5 Mbps wasted. The viewer called a frame stalled
  after 3 ms with no packet for it. Paced frames arrive over several ms,
  in Wi-Fi clumps.
- Worst moments: capture pauses again, up to 0.9 s (the game).

Fixed in m5-test8: the idle wait is 8 ms.
- A newer frame's packets remain the main signal: the host sends frames
  in order.
- A test: a frame arriving in clumps 4 ms apart is not asked for.
- losssim (video, heavy bursts 3.9 %): 13 frames lost and worst lateness
  28.6 ms. At 3 ms it was 11 and 25 ms; at 15 ms, 3 and 37 ms.

## Rate control (m5-test9, ADR 0027)
- The viewer backs off as soon as the frames' first-packet time rises
  15 ms over its quiet baseline, or anything is lost, to 85 % of what
  arrived. It holds at 6 ms over, and climbs back 5 % per 100 ms.
- The host's encoder follows it (NVENC, VideoToolbox, the service's
  helper), never above its own setting. Protocol v14: `VideoRate`.
- On a good link it should never limit ("no limit now"). Five unit tests
  cover the cases.

## A weak-link test mode (m5-test10)
The machines can't easily be moved, so the viewer can pretend its Wi-Fi is
weak:
- **The switch:** "Weak Wi-Fi test" in the viewer's control bar, on both
  viewers. It turns orange (amber on Windows) and shows "· 15 Mbps" while
  on. (The m5-test10 shortcut, ⌃⌥⌘L / Ctrl+Alt+Shift+L, was taken out in
  m5-test11.)
- **What it does:** what arrives goes through a pretend 15 Mbps bottleneck
  in core (`pacer::Bottleneck`). It queues when more comes, and drops past
  150 ms of queue, as a weak router does. Video and sound both go through
  it.
- **On screen:** "TEST: weak Wi-Fi, 15 Mbps", even with the stats hidden on
  the Mac.
- **Logged:** each switch on and off, with its time into the session, in
  the results and the running log: "test mode (weak link): on at 0:32
  (15 Mbps), off at 1:10 | N packets dropped by it".
- **Tested:** 45 Mbps in, about 15 out, the queue's delay topping out at
  150 ms, the rest dropped.

## Frame rate first, then coarse bitrate steps (m5-test11)
m5-test10 (PC): 63 bitrate changes, lowest 8 Mbps, and 68 IDRs: FFmpeg's
NVENC makes a keyframe at every bitrate change, and each one lands when the
link is already struggling. Now the host's first lever is the frame rate:
from twice the viewer's refresh down to its refresh (half the bitrate, no
keyframe, nothing visible lost). Only if that isn't enough does the bitrate
change, in coarse steps (80/60/40/30/20/15/10/8 Mbps), at most one down a
second and one up per 5 s. The PC's results say how often the frame rate
was lowered.

## The virtual display check (ADR 0028, before Round 2)
Windows only loads a driver that's signed. The question was whether Glass
can use an existing signed IddCx driver, since we sign nothing ourselves
(ADR 0008). From the projects' own pages:

| | Virtual Display Driver (VirtualDrivers) | Parsec VDD |
|---|---|---|
| Signed | Yes: SignPath Foundation certificate | Yes, by Parsec (the app EXE isn't) |
| Licence | MIT, open source | Parsec's own binary; redistribution terms not stated |
| 2560x1440 at 165 Hz | In its lists (640x480 to 8K; 60 to 500 Hz) | Not a preset (144 or 240); needs a custom mode in HKLM, then re-plug |
| How an app sets the mode | An XML file, then reload the device | IOCTLs to add and remove a screen; the mode by the Win32 display API |
| Other | `winget` install; admin | Must be pinged every ~100 ms or its screens go: tidy if Glass crashes |

Both need admin to change their modes. Glass's LocalSystem service (M3f)
has it, so it can write the viewer's exact size and refresh, reload the
screen, and remove it after the session. Nothing is fixed to one machine.

**Not settled from the pages:** neither says it's Microsoft-attested or
WHQL. A user-mode (UMDF) driver like these should only need a trusted
Authenticode signature, not Microsoft's, but that has to be tried: one
install on QUIETPC with Secure Boot on and no test signing.

**Plan:** the Virtual Display Driver first (open source, MIT, the exact
mode). Parsec VDD is the fallback. If neither installs cleanly, Round 2
streams the PC's real modes and the viewer scales.
