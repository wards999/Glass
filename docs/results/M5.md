# M5 — Smoothness: results

Status: **in progress.** Step 1 (the frame-rate cap) is built (m5-test1).

## Why it started early (m4b-test2)
The Mac (60 Hz) viewing QUIETPC22 (165 Hz) in a Steam game, 299 s:
- 75 fps arrived and 37.5 fps were shown: each frame was held for two
  refreshes, and `present` took 32 ms.
- capture → screen was 53 ms p50, so the sound (30 ms) came early.
- The decode queue was full 25 times: 39 keyframe requests, and 5 hitches
  averaging 138 ms.

## Step 1: at most one frame per refresh of the viewer's screen
- The host sends at the refresh the viewer reports in `Hello`, never more
  than its own screen's:
  - **Windows** (in-app and through the service): a frame that comes too
    soon is kept (a GPU copy) and sent when its turn comes; a newer one
    replaces it.
  - **Mac:** the newest frame waits in the encode queue the same way.
- The interval is 95 % of the viewer's refresh, so a new frame arrives for
  every refresh.
- The host's results say "frame rate (M5): at most N fps".
- Expected: the Mac shows close to 60 fps, the picture's delay falls, and
  the sound follows it down (its A/V hold, m4b-test3).

### m5-test1 (the Mac viewing QUIETPC22, 3D pool, 195 s)
- Better:
  - present interval p50 16.67 ms (was 33.3): a new frame every refresh
    when frames come;
  - queue full 0 (was 25), keyframe requests 7 (was 39), hitches 3 at
    30 ms (were 5 at 138 ms);
  - sound concealed 2 with 0 gaps (was 45 and 11).
- Worse: the user said "the pool balls felt less smooth this time". The PC
  sent only 29 fps (1,780 of 3,528 frames held back). Windows' default
  timer is 15.6 ms, so the cap's "send the held frame in 1 ms" took 16 ms.
  The Mac received 45 fps.
- Unchanged: capture → screen 54 ms. 32 ms of it is `present` on the Mac:
  a frame presented at the view's display link reaches the screen two
  refreshes later (the window manager composites it at the next refresh,
  then it's shown).
- The sound followed the picture, 24 ms behind (78 against 54). That's
  slow to settle while the backlog drains.

Fixed in m5-test2:
- **Windows:** 1 ms timer resolution for the session (`timeBeginPeriod`,
  per process on Windows 11). The cap now sends at 60. The sound capture,
  which also woke only every 16 ms, wakes every 2 ms.
- **Mac:**
  - frames are presented through `CAMetalDisplayLink` with
    `preferredFrameLatency = 1` (macOS 14), one refresh instead of two;
  - the layer is opaque, so full screen can skip compositing;
  - the results say "presented by: …".
- **Sound:** catching up a backlog is twice as quick (up to 2 %).
